# Local synchrony surfaces: revised Phase 2 production design

Status: implementation and experiment contract for one observed 90-day
Colorado window. This design stops before historical, CONUS, or multi-window
execution.

## Core correction

The Phase 2 scientific object is the two-dimensional local synchrony surface

\[
S_p(dx,dy) = S(c,p),
\]

where `(dx, dy)` is the displacement of center `c` from focal pixel `p`. The
equivalent polar coordinates are distance `r` and focal-to-center bearing
`theta`. Distance and direction are coordinates. A radial summary is one
projection of the surface; it is not the surface itself.

`max_radius_km=100` is therefore an **observation radius**, maximum support, or
maximum pair distance. It means “observe the local surface out to 100 km.” It
does not assert a 100 km characteristic synchrony scale. A feature that remains
unresolved near this limit is explicitly right-censored or unresolved within
the observation window.

## Scientific objects and three levels of spatial change

Phase 2 keeps these objects distinct:

1. Pair synchrony `S(i,j)` is one symmetric cold/warm/Delta relationship.
2. A local surface `S_p(dx,dy)` organizes all pairs incident to focal `p` by
   relative center location.
3. A center landscape `M_i(j)` is the spatial field generated by fixing center
   `i` and varying the comparison pixel `j`.
4. A candidate surface signature compactly describes a local surface only
   after its information loss has been measured.

Those objects imply three different notions of change:

- **within-surface structure:** variation in `S_p(dx,dy)` around one focal;
- **between-surface landscape change:** comparison of complete `M_i` and `M_j`
  when the center moves;
- **geographic signature change:** variation in validated descriptors as focal
  `p` moves across Colorado.

No implementation or report may merge these levels.

## Minimal CubeDynamics grammar

The production grammar is intentionally small:

```python
pairs = (
    pipe(climate_cube)
    | v.local_synchrony_pairs(
        lower_var="tmin",
        upper_var="tmax",
        max_radius_km=100,  # observation limit, not inferred scale
        output_mask=output_mask,
        window_days=90,
        min_t=10,
    )
).unwrap()

surface = (
    pipe(pairs)
    | v.local_synchrony_surface(focal_y_index=iy, focal_x_index=ix)
).unwrap()

diagnostics = (
    pipe(surface)
    | v.synchrony_surface_diagnostics(
        radial_bin_width_km=5,
        angular_bin_width_degrees=15,
    )
).unwrap()
```

`local_synchrony_pairs` is the bounded canonical relationship layer.
`local_synchrony_surface` recovers one complete audit surface with signed
displacement and focal-relative bearing. `synchrony_surface_diagnostics`
returns explicitly experimental radial, angular, boundary-like, harmonic, and
low-order reconstruction diagnostics; it does not declare a final signature
schema.

The existing `synchrony_signature(radii_km=(25,50,75,100))` remains available
as a **baseline compression method**. It is no longer the primary scientific
representation. `landscape_change_signature` remains a separate branch.

## Validated synchrony semantics

Cold, warm, and Delta retain the validated Phase 1 semantics:

- cold uses `tmin`, pair-valid lower quantiles, joint `<=` selection, and
  Spearman correlation;
- warm uses `tmax`, pair-valid upper quantiles, joint `>` selection, and
  Spearman correlation;
- pairwise missing filtering precedes thresholds;
- average ranks preserve ties and `min_t` is unchanged;
- `DeltaS(i,j) = S_cold(i,j) - S_warm(i,j)`.

The batched shared-mask fast path and exact pairwise-mask fallback are both
tested against `one_tail_spearman`. Optimization never changes the scientific
kernel.

## Canonical pairs and reversible endpoint geometry

Eligible pairs are found with a unit-sphere `cKDTree` and exact great-circle
distance. Each undirected pair is computed once. The pair table retains:

- cold synchrony, warm synchrony, and pairwise Delta;
- signed `dx_km`, `dy_km`, `dx_index`, and `dy_index` from canonical source to
  target;
- distance and forward bearing;
- joint-tail counts and endpoint indices.

The value contributes to both endpoint surfaces. For the canonical target,
signed displacement is negated and bearing is rotated by 180 degrees. Tests
cover exact values, offset reversal, bearing reversal, chunk invariance, and
tile seams.

## Surface projections and candidate descriptors

The full Cartesian surface is fundamental. Fine annuli and sectors are
numerical support for estimating continuous projections:

- `S_p(r)` can be nonmonotonic, flat, abrupt, or multiscale;
- `S_p(theta)` is evaluated both raw and after fine radial detrending;
- cold, warm, and Delta are retained side by side.

Candidate descriptors are evaluated rather than assumed:

- overall magnitude and heterogeneity: median, IQR, MAD, valid count, coverage;
- radial structure: fine profile, rank trend, turns, candidate plateau, and
  explicit censoring;
- directional structure: first and second angular harmonics after radial
  detrending;
- boundary-like structure: maximum robust half-plane contrast and orientation;
- low-order 2-D structure: interpretable `1,r,r²,x,y,x²-y²,2xy` basis;
- residual complexity: reconstruction RMSE and unexplained variance;
- empirical basis: held-out PCA/SVD across a broad Colorado surface sample.

Anisotropy and boundary-like contrast are reported separately. A strong
second harmonic indicates an axis-like pattern; a strong half-plane contrast
indicates a divided surface. Neither is called a climate boundary without
independent evidence.

Characteristic-scale diagnostics may return no scale, multiple candidate
scales, or right-censored/unresolved. No value near the 100 km observation
limit is interpreted as an identified 100 km scale.

## Compression experiment

On a reproducible, geographically broad Colorado sample, compare:

1. median and IQR only;
2. cumulative 25/50/75/100 km summaries;
3. fine radial profile;
4. radial plus directional summaries;
5. a low-order 2-D spatial basis;
6. held-out PCA/SVD;
7. another lightweight method only if evidence supports it.

For each candidate, record reconstruction error, parameter count, storage,
runtime, interpretability, chunk stability, and missing-pair sensitivity.
PCA/SVD is fit on training surfaces and assessed on held-out surfaces; training
variance alone cannot choose component count. Empirical components must be
inspected before interpretation.

## Storage architecture

Scientific and storage representations are separate:

- **every output pixel:** validated compact summaries and cheap candidate
  descriptors only;
- **representative Colorado sample:** complete aligned cold/warm/Delta surfaces
  for basis training and held-out evaluation;
- **selected audit pixels:** complete surfaces with provenance for figures and
  exact equivalence checks;
- **temporary tile data:** sparse pairs may be discarded after endpoint
  accumulation and selected-surface extraction.

Never materialize a dense `focal_y × focal_x × offset_y × offset_x` Colorado
array.

## Output domain, computation domain, tiling, and restart

`expand_spatial_domain` buffers the Colorado output geometry by at least the
observation radius in a local azimuthal-equidistant projection.
`spatial_output_mask` selects focal pixels to save. Political boundaries never
clip scientific neighborhoods.

Each restartable output tile uses a complete observation-radius halo. Canonical
pairs are unique within a bounded calculation; cross-tile endpoint pairs may be
recomputed in each endpoint tile. This changes cost, not values, and is recorded
as `cross_tile_pair_policy=endpoint_tile_recompute`.

Checkpoints bind source, variables, dates, geometry, coordinates, kernel
parameters, observation radius, tile bounds, schema version, package version,
and Git identity. Resume rejects incompatible or incomplete tiles.

## Gate and stopping rule

The 100 × 100 real-PRISM gate must verify kernel equivalence, complete surface
reconstruction, pair coordinates, endpoint reversal, chunk invariance, halo
equivalence, restart, and Phase 1/1.5 overlap before the Colorado run.

The Colorado experiment uses one validated 90-day window and a Colorado output
domain plus at least a 100 km computation halo. It samples full surfaces across
random, geographic, heterogeneity, gradient, direction, border, interior, and
tile-seam strata without treating those strata as climate regimes.

After the revised atlas, representation decision table, performance report,
and Phase 3 recommendation, stop. Do not proceed automatically to temporal
scaling or CONUS.
